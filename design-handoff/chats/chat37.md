Not really. "Gloom pop" is an informal descriptor, not a recognized genre with its own history. It shows up as a user tag on Last.fm, where the top artists include She Makes War (Laura Kidd) and other small acts, but critics and reference sources don't use it as a category.

The sound people usually mean by it already has established names: dark pop, noir pop, sad pop, or mood pop.

**When:** roughly 2012 to now, peaking around 2019.

**Who:**
- **Lana Del Rey** (early 2010s) is usually treated as the starting point. Vice called her "the CCO of the mood-pop revolution" and named her as an influence on Billie Eilish.
- **Lorde** is often credited for creating the new wave of dark pop. When her debut came out in 2013, she was described as an electropop singer, as much as she was labeled a dream pop artist, art pop, and indie pop.
- **Billie Eilish and Finneas** brought it to its mainstream peak. Her music is often described as "noir pop" for its world-weary tone and dark lyrical themes. In 2022 she was credited with marking the "ascendence" of alternative pop in the mainstream with her dark, downbeat pop.
- **Others in the same group:** Vice's survey of the trend covers Lana Del Rey and Lorde through Khalid and The 1975.

**Roots:** dream pop, trip hop, emo and goth aesthetics, and SoundCloud-era minimalist production.

"Dark pop" has been used two ways, and the Furs fall under the older, looser one.

**For the Furs, it's a description, not a genre.** Reference sources file them as post-punk and new wave. Wikipedia lists the 1980 debut as post-punk and punk rock, and "Heaven" (1984) as pop rock. "Dark pop" shows up in reviews as shorthand for pop hooks with a bleak tone. XS Noize's review of *Made of Rain* (2020) called them "an engaging amalgam of Dark Pop, Goth and Punk", and the same review called them forerunners of darkwave.

**Why the label fits them:**
- Richard Butler has long paired edgy, enigmatic poses and often inscrutable lyrics with fine pop hooks.
- One writer puts their sound near Bauhaus, Love and Rockets and Sisters of Mercy, but with a "devotion to pop songcraft".
- Their move toward pop came in the US. MTV and John Hughes films brought them a bigger audience just as their sound turned more commercial.

**How the two uses relate:**
- 1980s: "dark pop" was an adjective for melodic post-punk and new wave acts like the Furs, The Cure and Echo & the Bunnymen.
- 2010s: it became a working label for Lana Del Rey, Lorde and Billie Eilish.

I didn't find any source that traces a direct line from the Furs to the 2010s artists. The two uses share a name and a mood, but nobody has documented a lineage between them.
