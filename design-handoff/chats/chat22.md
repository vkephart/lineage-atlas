Yes. Eurodance is a recognized genre: mid-90s European club-pop built on a 130–150 BPM four-on-the-floor kick, rave-style synth stabs, a rapped male verse and a belted female chorus (2 Unlimited, Culture Beat, Snap!, Haddaway, La Bouche). It descends from house and Italo/hi-NRG and sits alongside trance as the commercial face of early-90s rave.

## Assistant

Yes, there are living descendants, but hyperpop isn't the main one.

**Direct line:** Eurodance → Hands Up / Eurotrance (late 90s–2000s: Cascada, Scooter, Basshunter) → **Hardstyle / Hard Dance** (2000s–now) and its current mainstream face, **Eurodance revival / Y2K dance-pop**, which is very much a 2020s sound: Kim Petras, Ava Max, David Guetta & Bebe Rexha's "I'm Good (Blue)" (a literal Eiffel 65 rework, 2022), Alok, and the TikTok-driven "slap house" / Brazilian bass wave. The 2024–25 **"hard dance" and trance revival** already on your map as Hypnotic Trance is the underground cousin.

**Hyperpop:** connected, but sideways. Hyperpop (PC Music, 100 gecs, SOPHIE) draws on Eurodance and 2000s Hands Up for its pitched-up vocals, cheesy supersaw leads and trance chord swells, but its trunk is pop, bubblegum bass and emo/nightcore internet culture, not the club. On your map it would be a hybrid node pulling from pop and electronic with Eurodance as a secondary influence, not a descendant.

So if you add Eurodance, the honest lineage is:

- **Eurodance** (1992, branch off House, tie to Disco)
- **Hands Up / Eurotrance** (1999, branch off Eurodance and Trance)
- **Hardstyle** (2003, branch off Hands Up + Techno)
- **Y2K Dance-Pop Revival** (2021, rising, off Eurodance)
- optional **Hyperpop** (2019, hybrid, pop + electronic, Eurodance secondary)
