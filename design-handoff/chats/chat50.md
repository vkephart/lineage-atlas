**What Dead Man's Bones and Marlon Williams share:**
- 1950s–60s ballads and doo-wop: Roy Orbison's operatic crooning, Phil Spector's echo-heavy production, girl-group chord changes
- Gothic or noir lyrics: death, longing, haunted romance
- A baritone or crooning lead with lots of reverb, and old-fashioned instruments rather than modern pop production

**Possible names:**
- **Gothic Americana**: an established term, but it leans toward alt-country (16 Horsepower, The Handsome Family). Williams fits; Dead Man's Bones only partly.
- **Noir Crooner / Retro-Noir Pop**: fits both best. It's less of an industry term but describes the music accurately. It would sit between the Rock and Roots families.
