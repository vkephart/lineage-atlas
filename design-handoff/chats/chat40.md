Hardcore Punk is already on the map (1980: Black Flag, Minor Threat, Bad Brains), and Rites of Spring is currently listed under **Emo (1994)**. The missing piece is the 1985 D.C. movement that sits between them: **Emocore**, also called first-wave emo or Revolution Summer.

**Research**
- **Scene:** Revolution Summer was a movement within the Washington, D.C., hardcore scene that took place between 1984 and 1986. Amy Pickering came up with it and named it while working at Dischord Records. It pushed hardcore bands to try more experimental music and to take apart the scene's toxic masculinity.
- **Year:** 1985. The Rites of Spring LP was recorded at Inner Ear Studios in February 1985 and released on vinyl in June 1985 as Dischord Records #16, produced by Ian MacKaye.
- **Band:** Rites of Spring was Guy Picciotto, Eddie Janney, Mike Fellows and Brendan Canty. They played only 15 shows. Brendan and Guy later went on to play in Fugazi.
- **The term:** Embrace was one of the first bands the press called emotional hardcore, though the members rejected the term from the start.
- **Its two branches:** Beefeater and Egg Hunt were early pioneers of post-hardcore, while Rites of Spring, Embrace, and Gray Matter were among the first emo bands.

**Related artists**
- Core: Rites of Spring, Embrace, Beefeater, Gray Matter
- Close orbit: One Last Wish, Dag Nasty, Hüsker Dü (influence)
- Later followers: Moss Icon and Indian Summer moved toward a post-hardcore/emo sound
