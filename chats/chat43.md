## Assistant

Anorak pop is better described as a scene and an attitude than as a distinct genre. Musically it's a strand of UK indie pop, specifically jangle pop. What set it apart was the image and the ethos more than the sound.

**Where the name comes from**
- "Anorak" is British slang for a nerdy obsessive, like a trainspotter or record collector. Many of the bands also literally wore anoraks, along with duffel coats, stripy tops and fringes.
- The music press of the mid-80s often used it as a put-down, alongside "shambling" (John Peel's word) and "cutie." Some bands, The Pastels included, took the label on themselves, half as a joke and half as a badge.

**What it sounds like**
- Jangly guitars, simple and sometimes deliberately amateurish playing, and soft or untrained vocals.
- Short, sweet songs, often about innocence or awkwardness, with a childlike streak.
- A DIY approach: fanzines, flexidiscs, small independent labels.

**Key acts and labels around C86**
- Bands: The Pastels, Talulah Gosh, The Shop Assistants, BMX Bandits, Razorcuts, The Flatmates, The Soup Dragons (early on).
- Labels: 53rd & 3rd (which Stephen Pastel co-ran), The Subway Organisation, Creation in its early years.

**Lineage**
- Roots: The Velvet Underground, The Byrds, Jonathan Richman, Television Personalities, and the Glasgow label Postcard Records (Orange Juice, Josef K).
- Successors: it fed into what became "twee pop," through Sarah Records in the late 80s and early 90s. It also linked up with the US K Records scene (Beat Happening and Calvin Johnson were close to the Pastels), and it carried on into Belle and Sebastian and later indie pop.

In short, "C86" names the compilation and the wider moment, "anorak" names one end of it: the fey, jangly, outsider-pop end. The other end was the noisier bands, like Bogshed, The Membranes and Big Flame.
