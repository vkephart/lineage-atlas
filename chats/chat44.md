Skate punk works as a node. It has its own musical signature, so it isn't only a scene.

**Why it counts as a node**
- **Rhythm:** the "skate beat", a fast double-time polka beat with snare on every offbeat. You can hear it in a few bars.
- **Melody:** sung, melodic lead vocals over hardcore tempos, often with stacked "oohs" or Bad Religion–style harmonies behind them.
- **Guitar:** palm-muted power-chord runs, more technical than '77 punk, with short melodic leads.
- **Lineage:** it comes from Hardcore Punk plus the melodic punk of Descendents and Bad Religion, and it feeds into pop-punk (Blink-182 and Green Day's early records) and later easycore.

**Where the scene part comes in**
The term means two different things:
- **'80s skate punk:** JFA, Big Boys, Suicidal Tendencies, Agression. Musically this is just hardcore tied to skate culture and Thrasher. That's the scene sense, and it belongs inside the Hardcore node rather than as its own node.
- **'90s skate punk:** The Offspring, NOFX, Pennywise, Lagwagon, No Use for a Name, and the Epitaph/Fat Wreck sound. This is a distinct sound and should be the node.

**Suggested setup:** name it Skate Punk, with the alternate names "Melodic Hardcore (SoCal)" and "Epi-Fat sound". Date it to about 1988–1991 (Bad Religion's *Suffer*, 1988, is the usual starting point). Parents would be Hardcore Punk and Punk. *Smash* (1994) is the album that broke it into the mainstream.
